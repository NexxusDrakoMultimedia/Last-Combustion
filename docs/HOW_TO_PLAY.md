# How to play

This guide covers the Garage owner career, the only mode built so far. It follows the game as it stands for 0.2.0. The rules and numbers behind it are in the [Game Design Document](GAME_DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md).

## The idea

It is 2031 and petrol is the rarest thing in the world. A new championship runs six rounds a season on closed Australian public roads, in ordinary road-legal combustion cars. You own a garage with one car and one driver and $250,000. Win races and the money, the sponsors and the fans follow. Run it badly and the money runs out: a garage that does nothing loses about $47,000 a season, so you have roughly five seasons to turn it around.

## Start a career

On the title screen fill in:

- **Your name** and **how you are described**, and your **date of birth** (you must be 18 to 50).
- **Garage name.**
- **Flags.** Every driver and garage has a flag icon beside its name. Hover over it for the nation's name.
- **Garage flag.** This is where your garage's main factory, founder, owner or executive office is. It does not say where you work.
- **Garage base.** Every garage in the series is based in an Australian state or territory. Your base decides your licence plates, which suppliers serve you and how far you travel to each round.
- **Two livery colours.**

Press **Start career**. You then choose a **starter car** from three brand-new Starter-class cars and a **starter driver** from five unknown drivers. Look at each car's model and each driver's stats. The cars and drivers you pass over go to the market and to the free agents, so you can still sign them later. Press **Confirm choice**.

## The screen

- **The top bar** shows your garage and the year, the date, your balance, the next round, the time controls (**+1 day**, **+1 week** and **Next event**) and one big button, **Continue**. Continue skips on to the next day something needs you, and on that day it becomes the next step: **Run next prelims**, **Run next final**, **End the season** or **Start next season**. The days and weeks stop early at any event, and wait while a race or the end of the season is due. **Feedback** opens the game's issue form on GitHub in a new tab, for bug reports, feature requests and anything else (it needs a free GitHub account). **Menu** has saving, loading, exporting and the About screen.
- **The garage floor** shows each of your entries: race number (with its borders), licence plate, driver, car and the car's condition.
- **The news strip** reports what has just happened.
- **The rail** on the right opens the panels:

| Panel      | What it is for                                                                                                                             |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Season     | The calendar, full results of every round (prelims and final) and every championship table                                                 |
| Cars       | The cars you own, their wear and value, full restorations, and your entries                                                                |
| Market     | Used cars, new cars from dealers, the auction house                                                                                        |
| Upgrades   | Fitting and servicing parts, and the road-legality score                                                                                   |
| Drivers    | Free agents to sign, driver training, gear, daily life and moving home                                                                     |
| Staff      | Mechanics and executives, your contacts, the season's social occasions and the press                                                       |
| Operations | The suppliers of electricity, water and fuel, their contracts and the spot market, outages, and the garage's facilities                    |
| Sponsors   | Your title sponsor                                                                                                                         |
| Almanac    | Champions and awards for every season, and the record book with leaderboards, including who has the most fans and the most attention flags |
| Game       | The save's facts, counts for the whole world and the series, your own record, and a dated chronicle of champions, your wins and deaths     |
| Registry   | A profile for every driver, car, garage, prelim and race                                                                                   |
| Ledger     | Your cash, income and spending by category and year, with a list of every transaction                                                      |
| Ownership  | Who owns each garage, its valuation, dividends, the board and takeovers                                                                    |
| Calendar   | The dates of what is coming: prelims, finals, social occasions and the end of the season                                                   |

Names, licence plates, race numbers, garages and rounds are links almost everywhere. Click one to open its profile in the Registry.

## Your first season

Before the first prelims, in any order:

1. **Sign a title sponsor** in Sponsors. A sponsor pays a season fee, a sixth of it after each round, and a bigger sponsor wants a more famous garage. You name a fee and a length of one to three years, and they accept, counter or walk away. A sponsor that walks away will not talk again that year.
2. **Hire a mechanic** in Staff. A crew cuts part wear and repair bills.
3. **Check your driver and car** in Drivers and Cars. Better free agents may be worth their salary. You can change your entry until the first round starts.
4. **Look at the Upgrades panel.** Parts add power or grip but each one counts against your road-legality score, and a car that fails scrutineering is disqualified.

Then press **Continue** to skip to the day of the prelims, and again (it now says **Run next prelims**) to run them. The prelims run over seven days: every entry sets one timed lap, and the 15 fastest reach the final. Press **Continue** to skip to the final's day and **Run next final** to race it. The **Calendar** in the rail lists what is coming. The final is simulated sector by sector; there are no race calls in this version.

Points come from the final for the top 15 and from the prelims for 16th to 31st. Prize money goes to the final's finishers. Pole and overtake bonuses add up through the year and are paid when the season ends. Open **Season**, then **Results** or **Standings**, to see every car and every table.

After six rounds, press **End the season**. The champion's prize is paid, the season awards are given out and everything is recorded. Then press **Start next season**: some drivers retire and rookies take their seats, contracts roll over, a few new garages open and a few fold, and the calendar starts again.

## The ledger

**Coming in:** prize money (the final's top finishers), your title sponsor, merchandise from your fans, delivery driving on the days between rounds (each of your cars earns about $167 a day that passes, more in a busy state), and the champion's prize.

**Going out:** each round, an entry fee, tyres, servicing, travel to the venue, electricity, water and race fuel; wages for mechanics; salaries for executives; driver training; crash repairs. Buying and fitting cars and parts is spending too.

The Ledger panel breaks it down by category. Suppliers charge the market rate unless you sign a contract in Operations, which fixes the price for one to three years at a discount the supplier agrees to.

## Looking after the car

Every chassis and every part wears. Racing wears a car ten times as fast as road driving, and a worn chassis is slower and breaks down more. In Upgrades you can service parts (cheap, resets part wear). The chassis can only be fixed by a **full restoration** in Cars. It costs as much as a new car, a quarter more each time, removes most of the wear, and leaves a minimum wear that rises with each restoration (faster for the fast, expensive classes). Once that minimum goes past 50, the car is worn out and retires at the end of the season, and the Cars panel warns you first. The button is greyed out when it would not help, as on a brand-new or lightly worn car, because the minimum wear would leave it worse off. Click it twice, because it spends real money. The car keeps its whole history.

Cars also have a lifespan. A car older than the limit for its class (25 years for a Starter, 20 for a Mid, 12 for a Premium) that has never scored a point retires at the end of the season and can no longer race. Score a point and it is safe, so watch the age of an old starter car you have not yet got a result with.

## Looking after the driver

- **Training.** Set a level for each driver in Drivers. It is paid at the end of the season and helps them improve and keep their peak longer.
- **Gear.** Suits, helmets and the like boost a driver's stats and wear out. You can have a maker refurbish an item for half its price in proportion to its wear.
- **Daily life.** Between rounds each of your drivers drives their car on everyday and delivery roads, which earns money and slowly trains them on the kind of road around their home. In the off-season you can move a driver's home for $5,000, from the next season.
- **Ghost record.** From your second season, the Entries panel lets you pick a ghost for each entry before a round's prelims: none, an easy ghost that runs two seconds ahead of you, or a hard one, the run a top driver from last season set on the same course. After the prelims the panel says whether you beat it, and by how much.
- **Course map.** The Calendar panel draws each course from its road, with a ring on every overtaking spot and a coloured mark where crashes, spins, mistakes, wildlife, failures and contact happened in the round you pick. A key under the map names each kind and how many there are.
- **Around the series.** After every final the press writes about the whole series, not only your garage: the winner, the big crashes, the rivalries that met and the stories that moved on. They are in the Press tab under Around the series. A crash in a final, or a prelim crash that wrecks an expensive car, makes the highlight reel: the driver gains fans (and fame) but loses a little reputation, on top of the repair bill.
- **Stories.** A rivalry that reaches a Feud becomes a story: the build-up, a showdown the next time both drivers race the same final, and an aftermath. When it involves one of your drivers, the Stories list under Rivalries in Drivers gives you a card to answer at the build-up and at the aftermath. Your choice moves the driver's fans and reputation, and how the two drivers get on. A card you leave passes after the next round, and nothing stops the calendar while it waits.
- **Rivalries.** Drivers who finish within three seconds of each other, or who lead the title fight together, build up heat. A pair that reaches Spark, Rivalry or Feud shows on the Rivalries list at the bottom of Drivers, with the meetings that built it, and a driver wins extra fans in a final the rival also races. Heat cools every round, and a rivalry ends when it cools or a driver retires.

## Saving

Open **Menu** and press **Save to slot 1**. The save is kept in your browser. **Load slot 1** brings it back. **Export .lcsave file** downloads a save as a file you can keep. A save from an older version of the game loads and is upgraded; a save from a newer version will not load.

## Troubleshooting

- **My save has gone.** Browser saves live in the browser's own storage, so clearing site data deletes them. Export a file now and then.
- **The game is slow to run a round.** Each prelim simulates 313 cars, so give it a moment.
- **A button is greyed out.** Entries and some deals are locked once a season has started, and money-spending buttons are off when you cannot afford them. The tooltip or the panel text says why.
- **A privacy prompt appeared.** The web build counts how the game is used and tells you so on your first visit (see the README). Press Opt out and nothing more is sent; Privacy settings, at the bottom left, changes it later.

## Where to play

Last Combustion is built for a computer: a desktop or laptop with a mouse and a screen of at least about 1024 pixels across (a tall 1080 by 1920 screen works too). It is not meant for phones or tablets, and on one the game says so once, with a button to carry on anyway.

## What the game does not have yet

Only the Garage owner mode, with one save slot and no race calls. There is no Junior Series yet, no story or interviews, no scouting or fog of war (every profile is open), and companies do not yet start up or wind down. The Game Design Document lists all of it, built and planned.

## Words you will see

- **Almanac:** the book of every season's champions, awards and records.
- **Game:** the status page for your save, with the facts of the world and the series and a dated chronicle.
- **Base:** the Australian place your garage works from. Not the same as its flag.
- **Black box:** the reigning champion's race number sits in a black box.
- **Entry:** one car plus one driver. A garage can have up to eight.
- **Equalizer:** the one technical rule: a car's widest allowed tyre is set by its weight.
- **Final:** the race that ends a round. The 15 fastest of the prelims start it.
- **Gold border:** shown on the number of any driver who has reached a final. **Silver border:** will mark Junior Series points scorers.
- **Headquarters:** where your garage's flag comes from (its factory, founder, owner or executive office).
- **Plate:** every car is identified by its Australian licence plate and the state that issued it. No two cars in a state share a plate.
- **Prelims:** seven days of timed laps before each final. Every entry runs one lap; the fastest start the final.
- **Prestige:** a garage's standing, from its entries, its gold-bordered drivers and its titles. It decides which sponsors, suppliers and executives will deal with you.
- **Registry:** the profiles of everything and everyone in the game, with a tab each for drivers, cars, garages and rounds. Each tab is split into Champions, Race winners, Active, Retired and Obscure, and each name shows the years it was active, such as (2031-present).
- **Greylinked (Obscure):** a driver, car or garage that retired or folded without ever scoring a point. It is listed apart and its links are grey, but its profile still opens.
- **Road affinity:** how good a driver is on forest, coast, mountain and night roads.
- **Wear:** from 0 (new) to 100 (worn out), for the chassis and for each part.
