# M7 plan: story and fog of war (0.3.0)

M7 is built on `feature/0.3.x`, made from `main` once 0.2.0 is out. It follows the same rules as [M6](M6_PLAN.md): one slice at a time, each its own pull request into the milestone branch with its tests, the Game Design Document updated in the same pull request and a line under `[Unreleased]` in the changelog, and one beta (`0.3.0-betaN`) for each slice.

Every design question that is still open, and every default taken without the owner's say, is kept in [M7 open questions](M7_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md), to be gone through before the release.

## Rules for every slice

- A save from an earlier version must still load: schema changes are new numbered migrations, and a released migration is never edited.
- Numbers go in `data/*.json`, text in `locales/en.json`, randomness from named streams, and each new data file is listed in [Data files](DATA_FILES.md).
- A new action that matters is reported through `track()` (categories, counts and amounts only).
- Every slice ends with the game still playable from the start of a career to the end of a season.

## The slices, in order

| #   | Slice                                   | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                             | Why here                                                                              |
| --- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Story and feedback                      | Rivalries, the storyline engine and event cards, media headlines beyond the garage's own, crash highlights, the ghost record, drone events and the live map ("Story and feedback" in the design document) | The people layer, the press and the calendar it reads from are built in M6            |
| 2   | Scouting and fog of war                 | Scouts, the Head of Scouting, and descriptions and ranges that tighten into exact values on every screen that shows a rating ("Scouting and fog of war")                                                  | It hides ratings on every screen, so it comes after the screens, and the story, exist |
| 3   | Racing by the day and the final in view | Every prelim day and every final can be watched, raced and managed, not only seen in a tactical view (see "Slice 3 in detail")                                                                            | It needs the calendar (built in M6) and the story layer's incidents                   |

Slices 1, 2 and 3 are planned in detail below. The work after M7 (the car industry, mortality and legacies, the 2036 expansion) is in [the M8 plan](M8_PLAN.md).

## Slice 1 in detail: story and feedback

The rules are in "Incidents, rivalries and storylines" in the Game Design Document. This slice builds them in four pieces, people and memory first, screens last (the race screens are reworked in slice 3, so the live map and the drone events wait until then). Piece 1 has its own step-by-step plan in [the rivalry engine plan](superpowers/plans/2026-11-12-rivalry-engine.md).

1. **Rivalry engine (built).** `data/rivalries.json` and a pure `src/sim/rivalry.ts`: the heat gains, the 10% decay each round, the levels (Spark 30, Rivalry 60, Feud 90, Legendary for a pair with five seasons of history), the end rules (heat under 10, a party retires, a reconciliation) and the cap of three live rivalries for a driver. Migration 0054 adds `rivalry` (the pair, heat, peak, level, start and end, end reason) and `rivalry_meeting` (the history of shared moments). `src/db/rivalries.ts` runs after each final beside `writeHeadline` in `src/db/final.ts`, reads the results and applies the round. The Drivers panel gets a rivalry board for the player's drivers and the fan multipliers (x1.1, x1.25, x1.5) feed the fan gains. Headlines that mention a rivalry wait for piece 3, which reworks the press. Property tests: heat never goes below zero or past its ceiling, the same results give the same heat, a driver never has more than three live rivalries, and a save made mid-season resumes to the same rivalries.
2. **Storyline engine and event cards (engine and _Rivalry feud_ built).** `data/storylines.json` templates (a trigger, up to five beats, an outcome), a pure `src/sim/storyline.ts`, a migration for storyline progress, the cap of three highlighted storylines, and event cards between rounds with choices that move fans, reputation and relationships. _Rivalry feud_ is the first template, because it reads from piece 1; _Rookie on the rise_ and _Underdog garage_ follow, as they need only results. What is built has its own plan in [the storyline engine plan](superpowers/plans/2026-11-13-storyline-engine.md): cards do not block the calendar (an unanswered card passes after the next round), and a choice moves fans, driver reputation and the relationship between the two drivers (prestige, morale and sponsor interest are recomputed from other state, so they wait for a stored adjustment). Rookie on the rise and Underdog garage, and headlines for beats (piece 3), are still to come.
3. **Press and crash highlights (built).** Headlines beyond the garage's own (the world's results, rivalries and storylines), and crash highlights that raise a driver's notoriety and cost reputation on top of the repair bill. What is built has its own plan in [the press and crash highlights plan](superpowers/plans/2026-11-13-press-and-crash-highlights.md): a new `news` table rather than a rebuilt `headline` (its kind check cannot be altered), notoriety as fame through the fans a highlight gives (so no new column), and a reputation cost with a setter shared with the story cards. The reckless-crash loss and further kinds of news (transfers, titles, retirements) are still to come.
4. **Ghost record, drone events and the live map.** The ghost choice before a prelim run, the drone signals as incidents, and the live map on the course outline. This piece is split, because the race is simulated in one batch today and a moving map or a drone pause has nothing to follow until slice 3 steps it. **4a (built):** a still course map in the Calendar panel with the sectors' overtaking spots and the incidents of a played round placed by their `km` ([plan](superpowers/plans/2026-11-13-course-map.md)). **4b (built):** the ghost record, a choice made before the round's prelims and settled after them, from season 2 (season 1's only ghost is a founder demo run that is not in the game yet; [plan](superpowers/plans/2026-11-13-ghost-record.md)). With 4b, the parts of slice 1 that do not need the race screens are built. **Slice 3:** the moving live map, drone markers and signals as incidents and pauses.

**Status: slice 1 is built** (pieces 1 to 4b, each its own pull request into `feature/0.3.x`). What it left for slice 3, because it needs the race screens: the moving live map and drone markers on it, drone signals as incidents and pauses (a prelim drone rule changes saved results for the same seed, and the design gives no size for the penalty of ignoring a signal), and the live ghost with the Easy ghost's hints. The beta for the slice (`0.3.0-betaN`) is a release step for the project owner (see `docs/RELEASING.md`).

**Calls made in this plan** (change them if you disagree):

- Piece 1 covers driver rivalries only. Garage, manufacturer and flag rivalries use the same heat rules and arrive with the championship screens later in the slice.
- Piece 1 applies only the heat gains the existing data can show: a finish within 3 s (`result.final_time_ms`) and a title-fight head-to-head. Moving to the other's rival garage and taking a rival's race number need transfer and number-change records the schema does not hold yet, and contact, final-race overtakes and press jabs need the incident and press-choice systems, so they join in pieces 2 and 3.
- The head-to-head composure and aggression change is also left to pieces 2 and 3, for the same reason. Piece 1 delivers the fan multipliers and the rivalry board.
- Rivalry rows and meetings use the world's drivers, so computer garages' drivers have rivalries too, and only the player's drivers get the board.

**Risks.** The heat rules read from results held in several modules, so the round update must run in one place and in one order or two saves of the same seed could differ; a save from 0.2.x starts with no rivalries, and they build up from the first final played (no back-filling from history, which keeps old saves loading unchanged).

## Slice 2 in detail: scouting and fog of war

What the player knows about anything outside their own garage becomes an estimate: a word, then a range, then an exact value. The simulation does not change, only what the screens show. Everything the player owns, and every public fact (results, points, numbers, titles, records), stays exact.

1. **The knowledge engine (built).** `data/scouting.json` and a pure `src/sim/scouting.ts`: a knowledge score from scouts (up to 50), evidence from scored finals (25), the subject's fame (15), the player's standing (10) and experience (30), the word band, the range half-width (20 down to 2, exact from 95) and where the true value sits in the range, drawn once per player, subject, season and stat from the stream `scout:<subject>:<year>:<stat>`. Property tests: a range always contains the truth, more knowledge only tightens it, it is the same on reload, and potential reads as knowledge 10 lower.
2. **Scouts and the Head of Scouting (built).** A Scouts tab in the Staff panel (a scout has a skill, a wage, a focus and can be assigned to a subject), a migration (0059) for `scout` and `scout_assignment`, and a ninth executive role that adds up to 25% to every scout. The scouts are built as their own table, with a wage of $20,000 plus $600 a point of skill a year and at most four a garage (the first guesses in the open questions below, change them in `data/scouting.json`); the assignment is by a call that takes a driver, car or garage, and the Scouts tab offers free-agent drivers for now. The Head of Scouting needed the `executive` table's role check rebuilt (migration 0060, its own pull request); the role is `head_of_scouting` in `data/executives.json`, and its bonus reaches the knowledge engine as `scoutLift`. "First to hear of rising drivers" is not built. Scouts do not yet appear in the Registry's Paddock tab, and have no age, so none retire.
3. **Fog on drivers (built, apart from mood, road affinity and the filters).** What is built: `src/db/knowledge.ts`, the `FogValue` component, the free-agent list and its salary hint, the negotiation hint and the Registry driver page, with a standing note in the Drivers panel in place of a one-time note for older saves, and the starter screen left exact (the recommended answers to the open questions). The Drivers panel, the Registry driver page for drivers the player does not own, and the hint in a driver negotiation, through one shared component that shows the word, the range bar or the exact value, with the knowledge score on hover and a note on what would clear the fog. Sorting and filtering use the range midpoint, never the true value, so the order does not give it away.
4. **Fog on cars (built).** What is built: car readings in `src/db/knowledge.ts`, the used market, the auction lots and the Registry car page, and the paid inspection (migration 0061, $1,500 in `data/scouting.json`). The fitted parts' wear and the performance numbers are not shown for cars the player does not own, so they have nothing to hide yet. The used market, the auction and the Registry car page. A paid inspection takes one car to exact for that sale.
5. **Fog on garages and deals (built, apart from the cap table and executive candidates' skill).** What is built: garage readings and the staff skill readings on the Registry's garage and person pages, and the fair-value hint of the sponsor, executive and supply negotiations as a range that narrows with relationship, scouts and past deals. Registry garage pages, and the other side of a deal in the sponsor, operations and executive panels, where a walk-away value becomes a range that narrows with relationship, scouting and past deals. Computer garages keep deciding from the true values.
6. **Other ways to learn, and polish (built).** What is built: attention flags (+25 knowledge for the season), watching races (each scored final counts as evidence for three seasons), former contracts and ownership as experience, telemetry (`Action:HireScout`, `FireScout`, `AssignScout` and `InspectCar`, counts only), an audit of the places true numbers reached a screen (the Game panel's richest garage and most prestigious garage were the last), and a documentation pass (how to play, architecture, the Game Design Document, the README).

**Open questions for the owner, as taken** (change them if you disagree): the starter screen shows the starter drivers and cars exact (it runs before there is a garage); a save from an earlier version gets a standing note in the Drivers panel rather than a one-time flag, so no new stored state; and the scouts cost $20,000 plus $600 a point of skill a year, with at most four a garage (`data/scouting.json`).

**Risks.** Ratings show in about a dozen files, so piece 3 centralises them or numbers will leak; with no scouts a rookie reads as pure fog, so free-agent choices must stay reasonable (a well-known driver reads at about 40 to 50 knowledge, a useful range); and knowledge is worked out for each subject on screen, so it is cached for each refresh.

**Status: slice 2 is built** (pieces 1 to 6, each its own pull request into `feature/0.3.x`). What it left: the skill of an executive or mechanic candidate on offer (the player needs it to hire), a driver's mood and road affinity, the cap table, the experience part of a garage's knowledge beyond deals, and "first to hear of rising drivers" for the Head of Scouting. The beta for the slice (`0.3.0-betaN`) is a release step for the project owner (see `docs/RELEASING.md`).

## Slice 3 in detail: racing by the day and the final in view

Today a round's prelims are one simulation with no decisions between days, and a final is simulated sector by sector with no calls. The aim is that **each day of the prelims and every final can be watched, raced and managed**, not only seen in a tactical view. The result stays deterministic: with no calls from the player, stepping through a day or a race gives exactly what the single run gives now.

1. **A steppable engine (built).** What is built: the final engine already played one sector at a time for the whole field with a pause for incidents, drawing each sector from a stream named for the race, the car and the sector, so it needed only plain-data `FinalSnapshot`, `snapshot()` and `FinalRace.restore()`; the prelims, which were one function, are now `PrelimRoundRun` (a day of prelims is one step, `PrelimSnapshot`, `snapshot()` and `restore()`), and `simulateRoundPrelims` is the same run played to the end, checked byte for byte against the old function. Tests: stepping to the end equals the single run, and a round or a final saved at any point and picked up again gives the same result as one never saved (property tests, through JSON as a save would carry them). The database table, the calendar's per-day events, the screens and the calls are the pieces after this one. The prelim and final engines become state machines whose whole state is plain data (`src/sim/prelim`, `src/sim/final`): a day of prelims, or a lap of the final, is one step, drawing from a named stream (`race:<round>:<day or lap>`) so the same seed gives the same step. Property tests: stepping to the end equals the batch result for any seed, and a saved state resumed gives the same result as one that was never saved.
2. **Saves in the middle of a race (built).** What is built: migration 0062 adds `race_state` (the frozen input of a race, the engine's snapshot and how far it got), and `startPrelims`, `playPrelimDay` and `playPrelims` in `src/db/prelims.ts` play a round's prelims a day at a time with the state saved in between, so a save made on prelim day 3 loads and carries on. What the round was started with is frozen, so buying a part or changing a driver between days does not change a round already under way. `playPrelims` is the same thing played to the end, and writes exactly what it always did (tested by saving and loading after every day). The round stays `scheduled` while its prelims are under way, so no `round.status` change was needed. The final works the same way: `startFinal`, `stepFinal` (a few sectors at a time, saved after each call) and `playFinal` in `src/db/final.ts`, with the finalists, the course, the day's temperature, the pole and the player's calls frozen at the start, and the results, points, prizes, timing, events and wear written when the flag falls (tested by saving and loading between steps and comparing every table written). Calls given on a final under way are set on it, which is what the managing piece builds on. The calendar follows: once a round's prelims are started, a `prelim_day` event stands for each of the seven (the dates are kept in `race_state.plan`, migration 0063, so the calendar does not read the large saved state), the final's date is fixed, each day is played on its own date, and a wear fix means the wear at the end is worked out from the car as it stands. Continue plays the prelims through by default, and a **Stop after every prelim day** setting in the Calendar panel stops after each day (the recommended answer to the first open question: stop only when something happens, with a setting for every run; for now nothing happens on a day, so the default plays them through). The final keeps its single event until the managing piece gives it stops. A `race_state` table (migration) holds the state between steps, so a save made on prelim day 3 or lap 7 of a final loads and carries on. The calendar's blocking events become one per prelim day and one for the final, and Continue moves from one to the next.
3. **Watching (built, apart from the live map and the drone markers).** What is built: the **Race** panel (`src/ui/shell/RaceScreen.tsx`) and `finalView` (`src/db/racewatch.ts`, from the saved race) with the timing tower, the feed, the weather and the controls, `stepFinal` stopping at an incident on request, and the store's `watchFinal` and `stepFinalWatch` (the round is settled when the flag falls, as when Continue runs it). The spectating view shows the whole field's timing as the race goes (the recommended answer to the third open question: results as they come in). The prelims are watched a run at a time too: `PrelimRoundRun.playRun` (checked byte for byte against the day-at-a-time version on four seeds), `playPrelimRuns` and `stepPrelims` (each run on its day's date), `prelimView` (today's runs, the classification so far with the qualifying line, the incidents) and the store's `watchPrelims` and `stepPrelimWatch`. The live map is built too (`liveMap`, `LiveMap.tsx`): the cars as dots on the course outline, placed from the gaps in the tower, with marks for the lap's incidents and drone stops. A race screen with a timing tower (position, gap, last sector times, tyre life, weather), a feed of what happens, and speed controls (watch each run or lap, fast forward, skip to the end of the day or the race). The course outline already drives a live map, which comes with the story layer's slice 1.
4. **Managing a prelim day.** Before each of the player's runs: the plan (attack, steady or conserve the car), the tyres and a repair or a retirement after trouble, each with a cost or a risk in the existing wear, tyre and legality rules. The computer garages decide from their profile (`ai_profile`) and the seed.
5. **Managing a final (calls built).** Calls during the race (push, balance or save, with Auto to hand a car back to the computer) from `final.calls` in `data/balance.json`, set from the timing tower for the player's own cars (`setFinalCall`, `callFinal`) and held on the saved race. A call moves a car a few places over a race, tested on several seeds. Still to come: pauses for incidents the player can answer, and defend and hold calls if wanted. The player can leave a race on automatic, which is today's behaviour.
6. **Polish.** Telemetry (counts only), the browser test for a watched day and a watched final, the Game Design Document's "Weekend format and management interface" brought up to date, and a documentation pass.

**Open questions for the owner.** Whether a day of prelims should stop for the player's decisions every time or only when something happens (recommended: only when something happens, with a setting for every run), how much the player's calls may change a final (a few places, not the result), and whether the spectating view should show other garages' runs live or only the results as they come in.

**Risks.** The engines were written to run in one go, so piece 1 is the largest piece and touches every race test; a state that is too big makes saves slow, so it keeps indexes rather than copies; and calls must never make a final unwinnable for the computer garages, which have none.
